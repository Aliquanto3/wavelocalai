# wave_local_ai_v2 Benchmark Suite

A reproducible benchmark that compares small language models running locally against cloud LLM APIs, across a shared set of task types, so a consultant can present clients with defensible on-prem-vs-cloud evidence instead of anecdote.

## Overview

Consultants advising clients on on-prem vs cloud LLM deployment currently argue from anecdote: public leaderboards score full-precision weights on cloud hardware and don't cover the quantized, CPU-offloaded MoE models now viable on consumer machines. This leaves both the consultant and the client without a credible basis for an infrastructure decision that carries real cost. The product closes that gap by producing hardware-bound runtime numbers and reproducible quality scores across identical tasks, run against both local and cloud models, presentable to a client during a pitch and defensible under a client engineer's scrutiny of the repo itself. It succeeds a working v1 (Streamlit + Ollama) and carries forward its validated carbon-accounting and model-registry patterns while extending scope to CI/CD rigor, security, and a wider task/model surface. The same evidence has a second life outside the client room: the published results are meant to support a citable write-up on running small language models on personal and professional PCs — a blog or consulting article first, an arXiv preprint optional — for which the repo itself is the reproducibility artifact rather than an appendix to one.

## Problem Statement

- Consultants need to compare local SLMs against cloud LLMs on the client's actual use cases, not generic leaderboard tasks, and be able to defend the numbers when challenged.
- No existing benchmark corpus covers the model class this project targets (quantized MoE, ≤4B active parameters, consumer hardware) — nor tiny dense alternatives that may outperform MoE on specific use cases.
- Results must survive three different readers at once: a client-side engineer auditing methodology and reproducibility, a client decision-maker judging the comparison at a glance — the latter through a presentable results surface, not the repo — and an academic or technical reviewer of a published write-up, who judges the method rather than the verdict and expects sample sizes, statistical treatment, roster composition and licensing to hold up on their own.
- Published evidence about small language models on ordinary hardware is thinnest exactly where a professional buyer needs it: a 16 GB office PC with no GPU is nearly absent from the literature, so a machine that cannot run a model at all and one that runs it acceptably are indistinguishable to anyone reading current results.
- Because this evidence is meant to run on a client's own machine or be handed to a client for their own reproduction, it must work unmodified when the project is duplicated on another machine, must not expose that machine or its data when accessed from a second machine during a demo, and must not send the client's own material to a third party as a side effect of being run.
- Choice of web-search tool and dense-vs-MoE model is itself use-case-dependent and unresolved industry-wide — the benchmark needs to answer "which tool/model for which use case," not assume one upfront.

## Goals

- A client or their engineer can independently reproduce the quality scores and verify the runtime numbers against the disclosed hardware fiche, with an explicit verdict of reproduced or not reproduced rather than an eyeball comparison.
- Coverage spans the full set of use cases: classification, translation, document comparison, email/text rewriting, code generation, agentic planning, agentic tool calling, web research, and RAG answer generation — with multilingual handling (at minimum English, French, German) carried as a language dimension of the classification, translation and rewriting suites rather than as a separate use case.
- For each use case, the benchmark surfaces whichever model family performs best — MoE or dense — including tiny dense candidates (e.g. Granite 4 350M/1B, Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B/4B, Phi-4-mini, SmolLM3, Llama 3.2) alongside the MoE flagships, not a single architecture assumed up front.
- For the agentic web-research use case, multiple web-search tools (e.g. a self-hosted option and at least one hosted API) are implemented and compared, so the benchmark also identifies which search tool performs best per model/use case rather than committing to one tool by assumption.
- Runtime metrics (TTFT, tokens/s, RAM/VRAM, energy, carbon, cost) and quality scores are both captured, kept separable, and each is defensible on its own terms.
- Judged (open-ended) scores carry inter-judge agreement between two cloud LLM judges of different model families, and no judge ever scores output produced by its own family.
- Energy/carbon figures are reported at a high, strategy-level headline (a single defensible number per run) always carrying the label that says whether it is an estimate or a measurement, while the detailed calculation and methodology remain available in the repo so a specific figure can be justified if challenged.
- Results are readable during a client pitch from a browser rather than a terminal: a service that never writes a row exposes the runs and their tables, and a dashboard presents them at the resolution a decision-maker reads.
- The pitch opens on an overview a decision-maker reads at a glance: one headline card per use case naming the best local model with its score and its honesty labels, the cloud comparator beside it, and the runtime and energy headlines, every figure carrying the same methodology labels its detail view carries. The overview is a read of published rows, never a new computation: it scores, ranks and derives nothing that the published rows and the published leader set do not already state. Epic: `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key`.
- A consultant can start a benchmark run from the browser during a demo and let the client watch it happen: one gated endpoint launches the existing runtime or quality CLI as a subprocess on the bench machine and streams its progress — the CLI's output lines, then the row once it lands — to the browser. The preserved invariant is that the service still writes no row: the CLI it launches remains the only writer, and a row produced this way is an ordinary CLI row. The endpoint is refused without the API key, disabled by default outside demo mode, and runs at most one run at a time. Epic: `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key`.
- A pitch audience can try a roster model first-hand through a model playground: a chat surface proxied through the service to the local llama-server, under that model's roster entry and its chat template, and optionally to a configured cloud subject. The playground is labelled on every screen "playground — nothing here is a benchmark row", and no playground exchange is ever written to any store. Epic: `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key`.
- The project runs and reproduces its results after being cloned onto a new machine, following only the setup documented in the repo, including via a provided container image.
- The repo itself demonstrates engineering quality (tests, measured coverage, reproducibility, pinned dependencies, automated security/dependency checks) as part of the evidence a client-side developer evaluates.
- A demo can be run from one of the consultant's machines and queried from a second machine, with access restricted via an API key.
- Changes to the benchmark are validated automatically before being considered shippable, and released versions are identified by a tag and an accompanying changelog entry.
- The published results are citable evidence for a written publication on running small language models on personal and professional PCs — a blog or consulting article first, an arXiv preprint optional — with the repo standing as the reproducibility artifact behind it: an academic or technical reviewer can trace any published figure back to the run, the suite, the roster entry and the machine that produced it.
- The same suites run across three declared machine configurations — a gaming tower with a desktop GPU, a gaming laptop with a mobile GPU, and a professional PC with 16 GB of RAM and no GPU — and, on the GPU-bearing machines, in a CPU-only compute mode as well as on the GPU, with the machine and the compute mode both carried as first-class dimensions of every published row. The no-GPU professional PC is an explicitly supported target rather than a degraded case, and a machine that cannot meet a model's declared minimum refuses the run instead of producing a quietly degraded number.
- Among the publication's intended results is a memory-bandwidth comparison the compute-mode dimension makes possible: the two 32 GB machines run the same suites CPU-only, isolating DDR4-3200 against DDR5-6000 on otherwise comparable CPU inference, so a reader can tell what system memory alone is worth to a machine with no usable GPU.
- Beyond model choice, the benchmark answers which inference engine, which agentic harness and which prompt variant serve a given use case best, each carried as a declared and recorded dimension rather than a fixed assumption.
- A published quality difference is stated with its uncertainty: a confidence interval on the score, and a paired significance test behind any claim that one model, engine or prompt variant beat another, so a reader can tell a real difference from suite noise.
- Suite items and the reference result bundle are published under an open licence and exported in a form consumable outside the repo, so a third party can re-analyse the published results without cloning and running the project.

## Benchmark Methodology

The rules below are what makes a published number defensible; each is stated so it can be checked and failed. The thresholds they carry — 20 items for a development suite, 300 items for a publication suite where the public source supplies them and a 100-item floor where it does not, N≥5 runtime repetitions, a 10% runtime tolerance, a 25% minimum share per language, a 10% judged subsample for judge calibration, at most 2 engines × 4 prompt variants × 3 agentic harnesses per campaign, 80% line coverage — are initial values, revisable by a later decision; they exist so each rule is falsifiable, not because the values are themselves evidence.

1. **Sampling determinism.** Every generated row records the seed, temperature, top_p, top_k and the exact model id used; re-running with those values reproduces the same score.
2. **Prompt parity and versioning.** Every row stores the final prompt string as rendered for that provider (after llama.cpp jinja templating or the cloud provider's chat templating), the prompt-template version id, the suite id, and a content hash of the suite's prompt set. Editing any prompt bumps the suite version.
3. **Generation caps.** Maximum output tokens, stop sequences, context length and thinking policy belong to the suite definition, are identical across every model compared on an item, and are recorded per row. These four are comparison constraints rather than merely recorded values: a published comparison whose two sides differ on any of them is refused under Methodology 24, which names the differing field.
4. **Suite size, level and language mix.** A suite declares which level it is built to, and every row it produces names that level. A *development* suite holds at least 20 items; a suite below that publishes its score marked indicative. A *publication* suite holds 300 items where the public benchmark it draws from supplies them, and never fewer than 100 where it does not; the suite records which of the two targets it was built to and why. Its items are sourced as subsets of named public benchmarks rather than hand-authored at scale: the suite records each benchmark it draws from, that benchmark's licence, and the rule by which the subset was selected, and every item drawn this way carries the contamination-risk marking of Methodology 5. The selection rule is not a seed alone, because a seed replays a draw only for a reader already holding the same source rows in the same order under the same loader. It states the canonical ordering the source rows were sorted into before sampling and names the stable source key that ordering is computed on; the loader library and its version, beside the sampler's own version; and the stratification keys the sampler draws under — language always, and the label as well where the source is a classification benchmark. Each drawn item carries a content hash over its own normalised text, so a third party can tell a re-drawn item from a re-used one, and any seed retry records the attempt count and every seed tried. A published table never mixes the two levels in one score without naming the level each row was produced at, and where the publication states one headline number that number is the development-level score, with the publication-level score printed beside it as the scale check: the hand-written development items are the only quality evidence the project owns that no public training corpus can have seen, and the publication-level suites exist to show that a finding survives at scale rather than to replace it. Each item in the classification, translation and rewriting suites is tagged with its language, and each of EN, FR and DE covers at least 25% of that suite's items. Language is both a composition rule and a reported dimension: the 25% share governs how a suite is built, and every published table breaks its score down per language, reports n per language alongside it, and marks any per-language cell below the cell threshold — initially 10 items — as indicative, saying so in the table itself rather than only in a footnote. The cell threshold is not the size target divided by the language count: at the 25% minimum share even a 100-item suite yields a ≈25-item cell, which resolves nothing finer than about 25 points, so a cell that stops firing the floor has not thereby become readable. What the threshold should be is a question for the threshold review, and removing an indicative mark from a cell is itself a claim, to be argued against observed interval widths rather than granted as a side effect of the suite growing.
5. **Item provenance.** Each item declares its provenance (hand-written, licensed, or public), and any public-origin item is marked contamination-risk in the published table. An item drawn from a public source carries its own licence beside that marking, together with the source it was drawn from and that source's revision: redistribution terms attach to the item that is redistributed, not to the suite that happens to hold it, and a bundle whose items came from two sources under two licences cannot state one licence for both.
6. **Runtime repetition and aggregation.** One server process produces one runtime row set. Its first generation is a warm-up, recorded as such and excluded from N; the counted repetitions are at least 5, run back-to-back, each separated by a fixed cooldown whose duration is recorded on the row (default 10 s). The aggregated metric set is named: ttft_ms, prompt_tok_per_s and gen_tok_per_s are each published as the median over the counted repetitions, with N, the mean and the sample standard deviation recorded beside it. Memory is published as the peak observed across the counted repetitions, never as a point sample, and every non-timing measurement on the row declares the aggregation it carries — which statistic, or which repetition it was taken from. A repetition that fails under Methodology 9 fails the whole row, naming the failing repetition's index and reason: it is neither dropped and re-run, which biases toward success, nor kept as zero, which destroys the median. The counted repetitions are kept in full, inline in the aggregate row as an ordered list, with the aggregate fields at the row's top level.
7. **Machine state and spread.** Each counted repetition records the machine state the platform can actually measure: GPU temperature and the NVML throttle reasons in force, plus CPU package temperature where a reader exists and an explicit unavailable where none does. System load is not recorded, because the platform value is emulated rather than measured and a fabricated number is worse than a declared absence. Spread is the sample standard deviation of gen_tok_per_s over the counted repetitions divided by their median; a row whose spread exceeds 10% is flagged unreliable. ttft_ms and prompt_tok_per_s carry the same statistic, reported for interpretation but never raising the flag.
8. **Reproduction verdict.** Quality is reproduced when two runs of the same model, prompt version and seed produce identical per-item predicted labels or scores. A cloud subject is judged under a stated tolerance instead, because a provider that pins temperature and seed still does not guarantee identical output: its reproduction is assessed per item, a re-run within the suite's declared per-item divergence tolerance is reproduced and names the items that diverged, and a cloud row that cannot be re-run deterministically at all is marked single-run indicative rather than not reproduced — provider-side non-determinism is a property of the subject, not a failure of the row to reproduce. Runtime carries three states, decided on one metric — the median gen_tok_per_s. It is reproduced when a reference row shares the re-run's normalised fiche hash and the two medians differ by no more than 10% of the reference median; it is not comparable when no reference row shares that hash, naming the fiche fields that differ; it is not reproduced otherwise. The verdict-blocking fiche fields are the llama.cpp build, the quant, the server flag set, the GPU and the compute mode — a `gpu` row and a `cpu_only` row from the same machine are not comparable and must never be read as a failed reproduction of each other. A verdict-blocking field that is null on either side makes the pair not comparable, naming that field; two unknown values never count as a match. CPU, RAM, driver and OS are recorded on the fiche and reported with the verdict but never block a comparison. The row records the run id of the reference it was compared against, the median ttft_ms and prompt_tok_per_s of both runs, and both runs' machine state — all reported, none of them deciding the verdict.
9. **Failed generation.** An item whose generation is empty, truncated, or unparseable scores 0, stays in the denominator, and records its failure reason; failures are never silently dropped from a comparison. Truncation is recorded as two distinct reasons: the suite's maximum-output-token cap reached, or the model's context limit reached. The first is a suite-level choice made under Methodology 3 and disputable as such; the second is a property of the model. A row that reports them as one cannot be argued with.
10. **Judge protocol.** A judged row records each judge's model id pinned under Methodology 12, the judge prompt id and its content hash, and the rubric version. The judge prompt is issued in the item's own language. The agreement statistic is named per suite: Cohen's kappa for a categorical rubric, absolute score delta for a numeric one.
11. **Judge independence and the judge pair.** A judge never scores output from its own model family. The judge pair is Z.ai's GLM and DeepSeek, each called through that provider's own direct API, chosen because both families are independent of every model on the local roster; Google and Mistral are benchmark subjects and are never judges. A subject independent of both judge families is scored by both judges and carries an agreement figure; a subject sharing a family with one judge is scored by the other only, carries no agreement figure, and is flagged single-judge. A calibration judge from a further family, GPT-5.6 Luna, scores a 10% subsample of judged items to test whether the pair carries a shared bias, in particular on FR and DE items: its result is published as two agreement figures of its own, one against GLM and one against DeepSeek, and is never folded into any suite's score. Every judge call pins the provider that actually answers — no fallback routing, and where a router is used its provider order is fixed, fallbacks disabled, and the answering provider written onto the row — records the reasoning effort it was issued with (disabled or minimal), and counts reasoning tokens separately from output tokens. Judge calls stay retryable and resumable: rate limits exist on paid tiers too, and a partially judged run resumes rather than restarting.
12. **Cloud model pinning.** A cloud row records the provider's dated model id where the provider publishes one, and otherwise its release or snapshot id — never a floating alias — and the API version. A run refuses to start when that id is absent from the provider's live model list, or, where the provider documents no model list, when a one-token probe to that id fails; the row records that the id was checked by a probe. Every row records each build marker the provider returns (a listing's version, name or creation date, a response fingerprint), and a run refuses to start when a recorded marker differs from the pinned one. A weight change under an unchanged id and marker cannot be detected; for judged scores, the inter-judge agreement and the calibration figure are the drift signals.
13. **Model roster.** Every roster model has an entry in a versioned roster file pinning its repo revision, file name, quant, checksum, its architecture (dense or MoE, and for MoE the expert count and active-parameter figure) and its own server flag set (a dense model takes no MoE-offload flags), and every row's fiche cites that entry. The architecture field is what makes the dense-takes-no-MoE-flags rule checkable rather than a convention. The published roster also carries a composition rule: each size class it publishes spans at least two model families, and where a size class has both dense and MoE candidates, both are represented. A size class covered by one family is labelled a single-family ladder in the published table, so a reader can tell a comparison of families from a comparison of quants within one.
14. **Hardware fiche integrity.** A fiche carries CPU, RAM, GPU, driver, llama.cpp build, quant, flags and the compute mode the run was executed under. Its SHA-256 identity is computed over a normalised projection of those fields rather than over them as captured: the model is identified by its roster entry id and checksum instead of a filesystem path, and host and port are excluded, so the same configuration hashes identically on a second machine and a moved models directory does not move the hash. The raw flag list is kept on the fiche as evidence and is not part of the hashed projection. Editing a fiche invalidates the rows that reference it.
15. **Energy and carbon.** Every row carries energy_kwh, emissions_kg, and the emission factor and region used to convert between them. The estimate-or-measurement label is per channel rather than per row: cpu_energy_method, gpu_energy_method and ram_energy_method each state how their own channel was obtained, and each channel's energy value is carried beside its label, so a composite figure is never labelled by the method of its smaller measured part. Every row also declares its emissions scope boundary — a local row is Scope 2 and excludes hardware amortization and facility overhead; a cloud row carries a Scope-3 estimate with the id of the formula that produced it — and any published comparison of the two states that they are not like-for-like until a local Scope-3 component exists. A runtime row's energy figures are measured per counted repetition — the tracker starts and stops around each generation, excluding the fixed cooldowns between them — and the row states which method produced them (energy_window_method: per_repetition_tasks, or unavailable when the tracker could not measure every counted repetition, in which case every energy figure is null rather than a partial sum) plus the active and idle window sizes (active_window_s, idle_window_s) it measured and excluded, so a fast model's cooldown never inflates its published energy, emissions or cost per token.
16. **Cost.** A cloud row carries tokens in and out plus an estimated cost from the provider's list price at run time; a local row carries an energy cost derived from a configurable kWh price. Both sides are expressed against one normalization unit, chosen once for the project and recorded on every row, so the on-prem-versus-cloud comparison divides by the same denominator on both sides. A row carrying a derived cost also carries what it was derived from — the list price value, its currency, and the timestamp it was retrieved — so a later auditor recomputing it can tell a wrong row from a moved price. Cost is reported, never optimised.
17. **Web-research archival.** Every search response is archived with its row so the score can be recomputed offline; a live re-run is labelled non-reproducible and is never compared against a published number.
18. **Web-research suite shape.** The web-research suite holds at least 20 queries with dated reference answers, scored by the judges against the retrieved sources, with the search tool used recorded per row.
19. **Run provenance.** Every row records its run id, capture timestamp, the release version and commit sha of the code that produced it, and whether that sha was stamped from a modified working tree — a sha taken from a dirty tree names code that never existed, and the row says so rather than failing silently. Every row also carries the schema_version it was written under; readers select rows by that version, and a store is never rotated or rewritten when the schema moves.
20. **TTFT provenance.** Every runtime row records ttft_source, stating whether the time to first token was reported by the inference server or measured by the client, under the same discipline the energy method labels carry. TTFT is a headline metric obtained today from an uncorroborated server report; the field makes that visible on the row instead of leaving two differently-obtained figures to be compared as if they were the same measurement.
21. **Machine dimension, compute mode and run profiles.** The machine and the compute mode are declared dimensions of every row, not merely properties of its fiche: a run profile exists per (roster model × machine × compute mode) triple and holds the flag set, context length and offload settings that combination runs under, so one model on two machines, or on one machine in two modes, is two named profiles rather than one flag set silently reused. The compute mode is `gpu` or `cpu_only`, and every fiche and every row names which one produced it. A GPU-bearing machine runs the same suites in `cpu_only` — every layer on the CPU — as a deliberately declared profile, never as a fallback: a `cpu_only` row carries no VRAM figure and declares it not applicable rather than reporting zero, since a zero reads as a measurement. Running the two 32 GB machines in `cpu_only` is what turns their memory difference into a comparison, DDR4-3200 against DDR5-6000, rather than a confound. Each roster model declares its minimal requirements — RAM, VRAM and disk — and the requirement check is applied per mode: a model can be runnable on a machine's `gpu` profile and refused in `cpu_only` for want of system RAM, or runnable in `cpu_only` and refused on `gpu` for want of VRAM, and each refusal names the requirement and the mode it failed under. The harness never substitutes a smaller quant, a shorter context or a CPU-only path on its own, because a degraded run published as a run is worse evidence than no run at all — `cpu_only` is a profile a campaign declares in advance, which is exactly what makes it different from a silent fallback. A machine with no GPU is a supported profile with its own flag set, not a degraded case of a GPU profile, and its rows are published as ordinary rows.
22. **Engine and prompt variant.** Every row records the inference engine that produced it and the prompt variant it ran under, each a declared dimension of the campaign rather than an option taken at the call site. The engine is named with its build identifier and the configuration defaults it applied: llama.cpp is the reference engine and Ollama the first comparator, and an engine whose defaults are unrecorded cannot be compared against one whose are. The prompt variant is one of baseline, constrained output (a grammar- or DSL-restricted output format), output compression (a terse output style), and input compression (an LLMLingua-2-class prompt reduction); a variant's definition is versioned like a prompt template under Methodology 2. One campaign declares at most 2 engines and at most 4 prompt variants, so the run count stays bounded and every declared cell is actually run rather than left empty. Whether prompt compression helps or hurts a small model — in quality and in TTFT — is an open question this benchmark exists to answer, not an assumption it encodes.
23. **Agentic scoring and harness.** The agentic planning and tool-calling suites are scored deterministically from the run transcript — the tool selected, the arguments passed, the number of calls made, and whether the task succeeded — in the manner of a function-calling leaderboard, and never by a judge alone; a judge may annotate an agentic row but never produces its score. The agentic harness is a recorded row dimension alongside engine and prompt variant, drawn from a named candidate set: `direct` — plain client calls with no framework — as the reference, and `smolagents`, `langgraph`, `pydantic-ai` and `llamaindex` as comparators. `llamaindex` is mandatory for the RAG answer-generation use case, where it doubles as the retrieval harness and so is the one candidate whose absence would leave a use case unmeasured rather than merely uncompared. One campaign compares at most 3 harnesses, so the run matrix stays bounded against the engine and prompt-variant dimensions it multiplies. Every row records the harness's own version and its per-call prompt overhead — the tokens the framework adds around the user prompt — because that overhead is itself one of the questions the publication asks: a harness that costs a small model a third of its context before the task begins is a finding, not an implementation detail, and it cannot be read off a total token count that does not separate it out. Agentic metrics are per complete task rather than per generation: tokens consumed, wall-clock duration and energy are summed across every generation the task took, and the row records how many generations that was, so a harness that reaches the answer in three calls is never compared against one that takes twelve as if they had cost the same.
24. **Inferential statistics on published comparisons.** A published quality score carries a confidence interval obtained by bootstrap over its items. A seed alone does not make that interval reproducible, so the row carries six values rather than three: the confidence level (95%), the resample count (10 000), the method (percentile), the seed, the identity and version of the random generator, and a versioned draw-procedure id covering draw order, tie handling and percentile interpolation. Percentile is chosen over BCa deliberately: BCa adds a jackknife and an inverse-normal convention to every recomputation, and independent recomputation of an identical number from the published bundle is the property this criterion exists to protect. Beside every interval, each suite publishes a minimum detectable effect per scoring kind — the smallest difference that suite size could have resolved — so a reader can tell a null result from an underpowered one.

    A claim that two models, two engines or two prompt variants differ on the same items carries a paired test, named per scoring kind: McNemar's exact test for a binary score, Wilcoxon signed-rank over the per-item scores for an ordinal or continuous one. The record states which test ran and why, the paired n, the tie and zero-difference counts, the p-value, the effect size and the formula behind it (rank-biserial for Wilcoxon), and the direction as candidate minus reference against a declared reference run id and candidate run id. Wilcoxon's zero-difference convention, its exact-versus-approximation rule and the threshold between them, and whether a continuity correction was applied are named on the record rather than left to whichever implementation produced it. A difference stated without that test is published as an observation, never as a result.

    The unit of the published artifact is the comparison family, not the individual comparison: a multiplicity correction means nothing until the family is declared, and a family enlarged after the fact moves every corrected p-value already published under it. One immutable family record is written per analysis invocation and superseded by id rather than edited; a family is by default one suite crossed with one compared dimension, and a family of one states explicitly that its adjusted p-values equal its raw ones. Each record names the field it compares and the fields on which its two sides actually differ, computed from the rows rather than declared: more than one differing field is an observation naming its own confound, never a clean test. A comparison is refused rather than published when its two sides differ on the suite identity of Methodology 2, on any of the four generation constraints of Methodology 3, on the metric identity and version, or on the scoring kind, and the refusal names the differing field.

    Wherever a statistic is undefined or would mislead — no discordant pairs, every paired difference zero, a paired n below the test's own minimum, a cell of one — the record publishes a named null reason in place of the number, on the same value-plus-one-reason discipline the agreement statistics of Methodology 10 already carry. A blank and a zero are each a false statement about what was computed.

    Runtime keeps the median-and-spread treatment of Methodology 6 and 7 unchanged: it is a repeated measurement of one machine, not a sample drawn from a population, and a significance test on it would claim more than the design supports.

## Non-Goals

- Fine-tuning or training models.
- Multi-GPU or datacenter-scale model serving.
- Production multi-tenant hosting or public internet exposure.
- Mobile deployment.
- Tracking deal outcomes or business KPIs.
- Authentication/authorization beyond the API-key-gated two-machine demo scenario (no multi-tenant user accounts, no public-internet threat model).
- Sending client-provided documents or prompts to a cloud provider, as benchmark subject or as judge. Only the repo's own suite items ever leave the machine in this release; any future opt-in must be explicit per run. The one opt-in this release carries is the playground's cloud subject: it is unavailable unless configured, and the playground states before each send that the typed text will leave the machine for that provider.
- Optimising or minimising cost, energy or carbon. The benchmark reports them; acting on them is the client's decision.
- Selecting one "winning" web-search tool or dense model up front — the comparison itself is the deliverable, not a pre-committed choice. What is excluded is a winner committed *before the evidence*, not a ranking the evidence produces: the leader set for a given suite and machine class — the models whose scores are not statistically distinguishable from the best under the paired tests of Methodology 24 — is a published derived output, computed from the reference bundle by the export tooling and recomputable by anyone holding that bundle. Publishing it is in scope; naming its members in advance is not.
- Wall-socket power measurement. Where a platform exposes no counter, CPU energy stays an estimate declared as such per Methodology 15; a wattmeter at the outlet is not part of this release, and the limitation is documented rather than closed.
- A lessons-learned write-up on AI-assisted development (Claude Code, and the AIDD and SpecKit frameworks used to build this repo). It is a separate publication and does not live in this repository.
- Multi-agent role-orchestration frameworks — CrewAI, AutoGen, and the vendor agent SDKs — as harness candidates. They orchestrate several role-playing agents against each other, which exceeds the single-agent tasks this benchmark scores; including them would measure the orchestration pattern rather than the small model running under it.
- A job runner. The demo run console launches one CLI run at a time for one operator and streams it; it is not a job queue, it schedules nothing, it keeps no run history beyond the rows the CLI itself writes, and it serves no second concurrent user.
- A product chat application. The playground lets a pitch audience try a roster model and nothing more: no stored conversations, no accounts, no file upload, and no result of any kind is produced there.

## User Stories

- As a consultant, I want to run the same task suite against local SLMs and cloud LLMs, so that I can show a client an apples-to-apples comparison.
- As a consultant, I want each run tagged with a signed hardware fiche, so that runtime numbers can't be challenged as machine-dependent noise without disclosure.
- As a consultant, I want quality scores kept separate from runtime scores, so that a client can't dismiss a good quality score because of hardware, or vice versa.
- As a consultant, I want open-ended task scores backed by two independent LLM judges with agreement reported, so that a client's engineer can't dismiss a score as one model's opinion.
- As a consultant, I want tiny dense models compared alongside MoE models on each use case, so that I can recommend the actually-best model per client need instead of a single architecture by default.
- As a consultant, I want several web-search tools compared for the web-research use case, so that I can recommend the best-performing one instead of picking one arbitrarily.
- As a consultant, I want to present the results from a browser rather than a terminal, so that a pitch doesn't depend on me running commands in front of a client.
- As a client decision-maker, I want to see the comparison as tables and a single headline energy figure, so that I can judge the on-prem-vs-cloud question at a glance without reading the repo.
- As a client-side developer, I want to read the repo and its test suite, so that I can independently judge the engineering quality behind the numbers.
- As a client-side developer, I want to clone the repo (or pull its container image) and reproduce the published results on my own machine, so that I can verify the claims rather than take them on trust.
- As a client-side developer, I want to reproduce the local half of a run without holding cloud provider credentials, so that verification doesn't require me to buy API access first.
- As a consultant, I want to query the benchmark running on one of my machines from another of my machines during a live demo, using an API key, so that I can show a realistic remote-access scenario without exposing it to the wider network.
- As a consultant, I want a high-level carbon/energy number I can show a client, with the full calculation available if they push back on it, so that I don't have to over-explain methodology in the pitch itself but can defend it if asked.
- As a consultant, I want to know what a client's own material costs me in exposure, so that running the benchmark on their premises never becomes a data-transfer question mid-engagement.
- As a consultant, I want every code change to pass automated checks, including dependency and security scans, before I rely on it, so that a broken or vulnerable benchmark never reaches a client demo.
- As a consultant, I want releases tagged with a changelog, so that I can tell a client exactly which version produced the numbers they're looking at.
- As an academic or technical reviewer, I want the sample size, the confidence interval and the significance test behind every published claim, so that I can tell a real difference between two models from noise in a small suite.
- As someone planning local AI on ordinary hardware, I want results for a 16 GB no-GPU office PC published beside the GPU machines, so that I can anticipate what a standard professional workstation will actually do rather than extrapolate from a gaming rig.
- As a consultant, I want the inference engine, the agentic harness and the prompt variant compared as dimensions, so that I can recommend a whole stack for a use case rather than only a model.
- As a third-party researcher, I want the suite items and the result bundle under an open licence and in a tabular export, so that I can re-analyse the published results without cloning and running the project myself.
- As a client decision-maker, I want the pitch to open on one card per use case naming the best local model, its cloud comparator and the runtime and energy headlines, with every label intact, so that I get the answer before the detail without losing the caveats.
- As a consultant, I want to start a run from the browser and let the client watch it progress, so that the client sees a number produced on the machine in front of them rather than taking a pre-computed table on trust.
- As a consultant, I want a client to be able to type a prompt to a local model during the pitch, so that they judge its speed and answers first-hand, without that exchange ever being mistaken for a benchmark result.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Given a supported task suite, running it against a local model and a cloud model produces one quality score and one runtime record per model, from the same suite items rendered per provider under Methodology 2 and 3.
- Given a runtime record, it always references a hardware fiche (CPU, RAM, GPU, driver, build, quant, flags) for that run, identified by the fiche's content hash.
- Given an open-ended task result from a subject independent of both judge families, it is never presented without both judges' scores and their agreement level; given one from a subject sharing a family with a judge, it carries the other judge's score only and is visibly flagged single-judge.
- Given a judged item whose two judges disagree beyond the suite's stated threshold, the item is published as contested and excluded from that suite's headline score.
- Given no client-provided document or prompt in a suite, no request leaving the machine ever contains one, and every row records whether its prompt left the machine.
- Given the full use-case list, each of the nine task use cases (classification, translation, document comparison, text rewriting, code generation, agentic planning, agentic tool calling, web research, RAG answer generation) has at least one task suite exercising it, or is marked out of scope for this release as a labelled empty row in the published results table; multilingual EN/FR/DE coverage is satisfied as a language dimension of the classification, translation and rewriting suites per Methodology 4.
- Given the model roster, for each in-scope use case it includes at least one MoE candidate and at least one tiny dense candidate, run over the same items with results shown side by side, and each of them cites its entry in the versioned roster file.
- Given the web-research use case, at least two distinct web-search tools are benchmarked against the same query set, with results attributable to which tool was used and every search response archived with its row.
- Given a web-search call that returns no results, an error, or a rate limit, the row records that tool outcome, and the item is excluded from the model's score while remaining visible in the tool comparison.
- Given a carbon/energy figure shown to a client, a single headline number in kg CO2e is displayed together with its energy_method label, and the underlying calculation (method, inputs, region and emission factor, formula/tool version) is retrievable from the same run's record.
- Given a cloud model row, it carries a Scope-3 energy and carbon estimate with its formula id and an estimated cost from the provider's list price at run time; given a local model row, it carries an energy cost derived from the configured kWh price.
- Given a published run, a re-run of it returns an explicit verdict of reproduced, not reproduced, or not comparable per Methodology 8, rather than two numbers left for the reader to compare.
- Given the repo cloned fresh onto a different machine, or its container image pulled, the documented setup steps produce a working benchmark run without undocumented manual fixes.
- Given a roster model, a target machine and a compute mode, a run profile exists for that (model × machine × mode) triple and every row and fiche it produces names that profile and that mode; given a combination below the model's declared minimum for that mode, the run refuses to start naming the requirement and the mode that failed, no runtime row is produced, and no smaller quant, shorter context or CPU-only path is substituted on the harness's own initiative.
- Given a GPU-bearing machine, running a declared `cpu_only` profile produces ordinary published rows carrying no VRAM figure but an explicit not-applicable, and those rows are never compared against the same machine's `gpu` rows as a reproduction.
- Given the no-GPU professional-PC configuration, it is a declared machine with its own run profiles, and its rows appear in the published tables as ordinary rows distinguished by machine rather than marked as degraded.
- Given the published container image, it ships no model weights, and its documented first run downloads the pinned GGUF by revision with a checksum verification.
- Given a reproduction attempt without cloud provider credentials, the local half of every suite runs to completion and the cloud rows are reported as skipped, not failed.
- Given a cloud provider failure mid-suite (quota, rate limit, or retired model), every row already produced is persisted and the run is marked partial, naming the failing provider and item.
- Given a code change, an automated check suite (tests with line coverage, lint, type-check, dependency/security scan) runs on the head commit and the platform blocks the merge until it reports success; the suite fails when line coverage is below 80%.
- Given a demo session, the service binds only to a configured address over TLS, a request carrying a valid API key from a second machine is answered, a request without a valid key is rejected, and no request from outside loopback is served without a key; the key is read from the environment, never written to the repo or logs, and the service refuses to start without one.
- Given a demo or a pitch, a results service that writes no row exposes the list of runs, the quality table, the runtime table with its fiche, and the per-run energy detail, and a dashboard presents those four views without the viewer touching a terminal.
- Given the pitch overview, it shows one card per use case: the best local model as the published leader set names it — every member when the set holds several, and a stated absence when no leader set is published — with its score carrying the same labels the quality view carries, the cloud comparator, and the runtime and energy headlines with their fiche reference and energy_method label; a use case out of scope for the release appears as a labelled empty card; a figure lacking a label its detail view requires is withheld and its absence stated, exactly as the detail view would; and no figure on the overview is absent from the published rows it reads. Epic: `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key`.
- Given the demo run console, a run request without a valid API key is refused, and a run request is refused whenever the demo-mode setting is off, which is its default; given a run in progress, a second request is refused naming the run in progress rather than queued; the endpoint launches only the runtime or the quality CLI, with a suite, roster entry and run profile chosen from the declared sets, never a free-form command or argument; the browser receives the CLI's output lines as they are emitted, then the row once the CLI has written it, or the CLI's exit status and failure reason when it does not; and every row is written by the CLI alone — the service has no write path to either store, verified by there being none rather than by convention. Epic: `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key`.
- Given the model playground, every screen of it carries the label "playground — nothing here is a benchmark row"; a request without a valid API key is refused; a local exchange is proxied through the service to the local llama-server under the selected roster entry and its chat template; a cloud subject is selectable only when one is configured, and the playground states before each send to it that the text will leave the machine for that provider; and no playground exchange is written to any store, result file or log line, nor appears in any benchmark view. Epic: `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key`.
- Given a release, it is marked by a version tag and has a corresponding changelog entry describing what changed, and every result row records the release version and commit sha that produced it.
- Given a published suite, its rows state whether it was built to the development or the publication level, and a publication-level suite names each public benchmark its subset came from, that benchmark's licence, and the selection rule that produced the subset.
- Given the published roster, each size class it publishes spans at least two model families, with dense and MoE both present where the class has both, and any single-family size class is labelled as such in the table.
- Given a campaign, every row records its engine (with build identifier and configuration defaults) and its prompt variant, and the campaign declares at most two engines and at most four variants.
- Given an agentic planning or tool-calling item, its score comes from a deterministic transcript check — expected tool, expected arguments, call count, task success — and never from a judge's opinion; the row names the harness used and its version, reports that harness's per-call prompt overhead separately from the task's own tokens, and reports tokens, duration and energy for the complete task alongside the number of generations it took; the campaign compares at most three harnesses, and a RAG answer-generation campaign includes `llamaindex` among them.
- Given a published quality score, it is shown with a bootstrap confidence interval; given a claim that two models, engines or prompt variants differ, it is shown with a paired Wilcoxon test's p-value and effect direction, or it is not presented as a difference.
- Given a judged item, its row names the judge provider that actually answered, the reasoning effort the call was issued with, and its reasoning-token count separately from its output tokens; given the calibration subsample, its agreement with each judge of the pair is published as its own figure and no suite score moves because of it.
- Given a cloud subject re-run, its quality reproduction verdict is decided per item under the declared divergence tolerance and names the items that diverged; given a cloud subject that cannot be re-run deterministically, its row is marked single-run indicative rather than not reproduced.
- Given a published release, its suite items and reference result bundle carry CC-BY 4.0 while the code stays MIT, and the published results are also available as a tabular export readable outside the repo without running it.
- Given a benchmark result shown to a client or their engineer, the consultant logs in a tracked file whether it was challenged, dismissed, or accepted, with the challenger's role, the evidence offered, and the acceptance criterion disputed; a challenge counts as sustained when it is not resolved by evidence within that session. After at least 3 such logged sessions with no sustained challenge to fiche disclosure, table separation, or judge agreement, the artifact is considered validated as credible for that release; elapsed time alone never validates it, and any sustained challenge is logged as a follow-up item rather than silently accepted.

## Dependencies

- Paid, low-cost direct API access to the two judge providers (Z.ai for GLM, DeepSeek for DeepSeek) and to the calibration judge's provider. A small judging budget, on the order of ten dollars per campaign at catalogue rates, is accepted, so the free-tier-only constraint no longer holds; rate-limit survival — retry and partial resume — still does, because paid tiers are rate-limited too.
- Continued free-tier or low-cost access to at least one independent cloud LLM provider as a benchmark *subject*, for the on-prem-versus-cloud comparison itself.
- Continued availability of the three target machine configurations — a desktop-GPU tower, a mobile-GPU laptop, and a 16 GB no-GPU professional PC — since the machine is a published dimension and a missing machine leaves a hole in every comparison that cites it.
- Licence and redistribution terms of the public benchmarks whose subsets seed the publication-level suites, permitting those subsets to be republished alongside the CC-BY 4.0 reference bundle, under their own licence where it is not CC-BY 4.0.
- Availability of a second inference engine (Ollama) as a pinned, installable build alongside llama.cpp, since the engine comparison has no content without one.
- Continued free or low-cost access to at least two web-search tools/APIs for the web-research use case comparison.
- Availability of local hardware representative of the target consumer-hardware class (CPU offload, ≤20GB@Q4).
- Obtainability of the roster's model weights and of the local inference runtime: each GGUF named by its source repository and revision with a checksum, and the llama.cpp build named by tag with its download source. Without both, no run starts and no reproduction is possible.
- Licence terms of each roster model permitting the client-side use the reproduction story asks for; a roster entry records the licence and whether client-side commercial use is permitted.
- GitHub (or equivalent) as the hosting and distribution point clients and their engineers use to access the repo, and as the CI/CD execution environment.
- CodeCarbon's offline-mode measurement approach (physical, Scope 2) for local runs, requiring a declared region and emission factor, paired with a calibrated formula-based estimate (Scope 3, covering datacenter overhead and hardware amortization) for cloud/API models — both patterns already validated in the v1 predecessor and carried forward.
- A container registry (or equivalent) to distribute the release image referenced in the reproducibility acceptance criteria.

## Open Questions

- Whether the Windows/NVML energy-estimate caveat needs specific client-facing disclosure wording beyond the mandatory energy_method label, or the high-level/detailed-calc split above is sufficient disclosure on its own.
- Whether a third judging family is added so that a subject drawn from one of the judge families regains an agreement figure instead of the single-judge flag, or whether such subjects simply stay off the roster.
- Which specific web-search tools are selected as the initial comparison set (candidates identified: a self-hosted option and one or more hosted APIs) — a technical decision deferred out of this PRD, but the number and identity of tools affects how "web research" acceptance criteria get validated.
- Which specific dense and MoE models make the initial roster per use case — deferred as a technical/model-selection decision, not fixed here.
- Exact scope and enforcement level of the automated security/dependency scanning (which findings block a merge vs. only get logged) — v1 ran these as non-blocking; whether v2 makes any of them blocking is undecided.
- Whether the Methodology's initial thresholds (20 development items, 300 publication items where the public source supplies them and 100 where it does not, N≥5, 10%, 25% per language, a 10-item per-language cell threshold, a 10% calibration subsample, 80% coverage) survive the first full-roster run, or need to move once real spread and real suite sizes are known. The per-language cell threshold is the one most likely to move: at the 25% minimum share it governs cells of ≈25 to 75 items, and the review that changes it has to cite observed interval widths and cell counts rather than the fact that the suite grew.
- Whether "credible artifact" validation (per the acceptance criterion above) needs a firmer, product-wide check-in cadence beyond the per-session log.
- Whether prompt compression helps or hurts a small model, in quality and in TTFT, and whether the answer differs for agentic work — the benchmark's own open research question. The one outside figure in hand, roughly an 8.5% token saving with no measured quality loss on a coding agent, is a hypothesis to test, not a result to carry.
- Which public benchmarks the publication-level suites draw their subsets from, and what selection rule keeps a subset representative rather than merely convenient. The licence question this settles has three outcomes rather than two, each carrying a consequence written before the answer arrives: *permissive* republishes the drawn items under their source's own permissive licence (CC-BY 4.0, or one such as Apache-2.0 that lets them sit inside the CC-BY 4.0 bundle), with the licence, source and revision recorded per item; *share-alike* republishes them under the source's own terms, stated per item, so the bundle's licence becomes per-item rather than uniform; *no redistribution* publishes item ids, content hashes and scores without the item text, and the affected level is reported as not independently re-runnable without the source. The outcome is evaluated against the row contract of Methodology 4 and 5 — which fields a redistributed row must carry — rather than against the shape of any directory in the repository.
- Which three of the five candidate harnesses a given campaign compares, now that the candidate set itself is fixed, and whether the harness comparison runs on every agentic model or only on those that pass the direct-call suite.
- Whether each release also receives an archival DOI (Zenodo or equivalent). Desirable for citation, deliberately optional for this release, and not an acceptance criterion.
- Whether a software-paper venue is targeted after the article and the optional preprint. It would add requirements to the repo itself and is not decided here.
- Whether the playground gains a RAG variant, answering over the repo-owned retrieval corpus, and an agentic variant, calling tools on the model's behalf. Both are deferred until the use-case epic (`no-use-case-is-silently-absent`) ships the retrieval and tool-calling capabilities they would stand on; neither is an acceptance criterion of this release.
- Whether the pitch overview may show the highest published local score, labelled as an observation, for a use case whose leader set is not yet published, or whether that card stays a stated absence until the paired tests of Methodology 24 have run. The acceptance criterion takes the strict reading; relaxing it is a product decision, not a presentation tweak.
