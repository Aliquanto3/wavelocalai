# Spike: Which public classification benchmark seeds the publication suite, and on what terms

## Question

Which named public classification benchmark can seed a publication-level suite of at least 100 items (300 where it supplies them) with EN, FR and DE each at 25% or more, and does its licence put a drawn subset on the permissive, share-alike or no-redistribution rung for a bundle published under CC-BY 4.0?

## Decision

Which source the classification publication suite draws from, and which of the epic's three pre-written licence consequences applies to its items and derived rows: permissive ships items and rows unchanged; share-alike segregates the drawn items and their rows under their own licence file; no-redistribution redacts `prompt` and `expected_label` on the rows to the per-item content hash.

## Bounds

- Evidence needed: for each lead (MASSIVE first, as the epic names it; others only if it fails), the licence text as published by the source at a named revision; whether that licence is share-alike, checked first because it is the likeliest branch; whether it permits redistributing a subset inside a CC-BY 4.0 bundle, evaluated against the row contract (`REQUIRED_FIELDS["quality"]` carries `prompt` and `expected_label` verbatim), not only against `suite-definitions/`; whether EN, FR and DE splits exist at the needed size with a label set usable for exact-match scoring; the stable source key and loader a canonical ordering would use.
- Stop when: one lead is shown to meet the size and language constraints with its licence rung stated from the licence text, or every named lead is shown to fail one of them.

### Evidence

- MASSIVE 1.1 is CC BY 4.0 at its publisher (Amazon), in the HF repo `LICENSE` at a pinned revision and in the GitHub NOTICE, and its SLURP seed text is CC BY 4.0 too. It is not share-alike, not NC, not ND.
- MInDS-14 is CC BY 4.0 by its publisher's (PolyAI's) HF card at a pinned revision, stated as a licence identifier plus the prose "Creative Commons license (CC-BY)". There is no licence file in the HF repo, and the paper is silent on licence.
- Both CC BY 4.0 sources grant "reproduce and Share the Licensed Material, in whole or in part" and "produce, reproduce, and Share Adapted Material" (Section 2(a)(1)), subject to attribution (Section 3(a)). This covers a row's `prompt` (item text inside a project-authored instruction) and its `expected_label` verbatim, and covers the suite definition. A CC-BY 4.0 bundle adds no term that restricts recipients (Section 2(a)(5)(b)), so the drawn items and rows can sit inside it.
- Size and language: MASSIVE `test` has 2,974 rows per locale, and its smallest `fr` scenario cell is 57, against a need of 6. MInDS-14 has 539 to 611 rows per locale, and its smallest cell is 31, against a need of 8. Both supply 300 items (100 per language), so the 300 target applies to either.
- Label sets: MASSIVE's 18 scenarios and 60 intents are voice-assistant domains (alarm, music, weather, ...). None overlaps support routing. MInDS-14's 14 intents are e-banking customer-service requests (card_issues, app_error, pay_bill, address, ...), the closest public analogue found to `classification-support-routing` (billing, technical, account, other). Both are closed sets usable for exact-label-match.
- Text character: MASSIVE FR and DE are crowd localisations of English SLURP seeds (parallel, lower-case, unpunctuated). MInDS-14 FR and DE were spoken natively per language and reach the text as ASR 1-best output, so some items carry recognition errors.
- Leads that fail: SIB-200 and MTOP are share-alike. The Amazon Reviews corpus is no-redistribution and barred for consultancy use. Tobi-Bueck tickets are NC and lack FR. BANKING77 is EN only.

### Decisions

- The epic's three-rung ladder (Decisions table, "The licence spike returns one of three verdicts, each with its consequence written in advance"): permissive ships items and rows unchanged.
- The epic's dependency row names MASSIVE as a lead, "Leads, not choices".
- Q41 (a), answered 2026-10-01, governs only the no-redistribution rung. It is not triggered here.
- Q105 (a), answered 2026-10-03: MInDS-14 seeds the suite, 300 items, 100 per language, stratified by intent.

### Assumptions

- PolyAI's HF card is the publisher's licence statement for MInDS-14. The `MInDS-14.zip` original release was not opened (reading it would be a download), so whether it carries its own licence file is unchecked.
- Google Cloud Speech-to-Text output imposes no redistribution restriction on the transcriptions PolyAI published under CC BY 4.0. The spike did not read Google's terms.
- `path` is unique across MInDS-14. The sampler would refuse a duplicate at the first draw, so a wrong assumption fails loudly.
- An EN stratum drawn from `en-US` only (563 rows) is enough. `en-GB` and `en-AU` could join it under language `en` without changing any conclusion.

## Outcome

- Result: MInDS-14 seeds the classification publication suite on the **permissive** rung (owner answer Q105 (a), 2026-10-03). Every candidate that meets size and language under a usable licence is CC BY 4.0, so the classification publication suite is on the **permissive** rung: items and derived rows ship unchanged inside the CC-BY 4.0 bundle, with attribution. Two sources qualify. (1) **MInDS-14** (`PolyAI/minds14` @ `40ce77cb32a384e4d50a568e1ec39ac804019d33`, CC-BY-4.0): configs `en-US`, `fr-FR`, `de-DE`; stable source key `path`; label field `intent_class` mapped to its name (14 labels); content fields `transcription` and the label; loader any `datasets` 4.x, or pyarrow over the three parquet files without the `audio` column. At 300 items, each language holds 100, with 8 per intent for the first two labels in sorted order and 7 for the rest. (2) **MASSIVE 1.1** (`AmazonScience/massive` @ `ff6bd8e4b27c3543e4f8fe2108f32bb95a6f8740`, CC-BY-4.0, Copyright Amazon.com Inc. or its affiliates; SLURP text CC BY 4.0): `test` partition of `en-US`, `fr-FR`, `de-DE`, label field `scenario` (18 labels, 5 or 6 per label per language; the 60 intents would leave 1 or 2 items per intent). Two constraints apply to MASSIVE. Its `id` repeats across locales, so the stable key must be a loader-computed `locale/id`, or the sampler refuses the source. Its HF loader is a script that `datasets` 4.x cannot run, so the loader reads `amazon-massive-dataset-1.1.tar.gz` directly and records that file's SHA-256 beside the HF revision. A parquet mirror such as `mteb/amazon_massive_scenario` mislabels the licence as apache-2.0, and an item's licence must still read CC-BY-4.0. MInDS-14 fits the support-routing construct the story's "So that" asks the scale check to test. MASSIVE has the stronger licence paper trail and is the epic's named lead. Neither dominates on every criterion; the owner chose MInDS-14 for its fit to the support-routing construct, accepting three costs: its licence evidence is the card's identifier and a prose line, not a licence file; ASR noise lowers the exact-match ceiling; and the pool is small enough that a later redraw overlaps.
- Confidence: high on the rung, which is CC BY 4.0 at the publisher for both, read directly. High on sizes and label sets (Viewer statistics). Medium on the MInDS-14 licence, which rests on the card's identifier and an unversioned prose line, not a licence file.
- Remaining uncertainty: whether the original `MInDS-14.zip` carries a licence file that differs from the card. Whether MASSIVE's S3 tarball is still served no longer matters.
