# Spike: Which public translation benchmark seeds the publication suite, and on what terms

## Question

Which named public translation benchmark can seed a publication-level suite of at least 100 items (300 where it supplies them) with EN, FR and DE each at 25% or more and a reference translation per item, and does its licence put a drawn subset on the permissive, share-alike or no-redistribution rung for a bundle published under CC-BY 4.0?

## Decision

Which source the translation publication suite draws from, and which of the epic's three pre-written licence consequences applies to its items and derived rows: permissive ships items and rows unchanged; share-alike segregates the drawn items and their rows under their own licence file; no-redistribution redacts `prompt` and `reference_output` on the rows to the per-item content hash.

## Bounds

- Evidence needed: for each lead (FLORES-200 and a WMT test set, as the epic names them; others only if both fail), the licence text as published by the source at a named revision; whether that licence is share-alike, checked first because it is the likeliest branch; whether it permits redistributing a subset inside a CC-BY 4.0 bundle, evaluated against the row contract (`GRADED_FIELDS` carries `reference_output`, and `prompt` is required on every quality row), not only against `suite-definitions/`; whether the EN, FR and DE directions the suite needs exist at the needed size; the stable source key and loader a canonical ordering would use.
- Stop when: one lead is shown to meet the size and direction constraints with its licence rung stated from the licence text, or every named lead is shown to fail one of them.

## Outcome

- Status note (caller, 2026-10-03): the permissive rung rests on assumption A1, Google's Apache-2.0 declaration over English source text that WMT itself released "for research purposes" only. Accepting that declaration for a public repository was a risk only the owner could take, filed as Q106 in `aidd_docs/tasks/2026_10/2026_10_02_backlog-refinement/owner-questions.md`. The owner accepted it (owner answer Q106 (a), 2026-10-03): items ship with `licence` `Apache-2.0`, the Apache-2.0 text ships beside them, and `LICENSE-DATA` section 3 discloses the WMT24 research-use origin as a declaration, on the same footing as the two declarations already there. If Google's label is ever withdrawn, the items move to the no-redistribution rung under the supersede-don't-backfill discipline. The spike is `resolved`; everything else below stands.
- Result: WMT24++ (`google/wmt24pp`, revision `fd7405c06494bc66a57b25f55d217a72f96e60dc`), the WMT lead, seeds the translation publication suite on the **permissive** rung. Its published licence is Apache-2.0, which is neither share-alike nor no-redistribution; a drawn item and its derived rows (`prompt`, `reference_output`) ship unchanged with `licence` `Apache-2.0`, `source` `google/wmt24pp` and `source_revision` `fd7405c0...` per item. This does not collide with the bundle's CC-BY 4.0 because `LICENSE-DATA` section 2 already excludes drawn items from the CC-BY 4.0 grant and makes their licence per-item (Methodology 5). Size and directions: 998 aligned segments per pair minus the canary and `is_bad_source` rows; joining `en-fr_FR` and `en-de_DE` on `segment_id` yields EN->FR, FR->DE and DE->EN, the hand-written suite's three directions, so 100 distinct segments per direction gives the 300-item target with EN, FR and DE at 33% each as source language. Stable source key: `segment_id` (int64, shared across the pair files); canonical ordering: ascending `segment_id` at the pinned revision; loader: the Hugging Face `datasets` library (or `huggingface_hub` reading the two jsonl files) pinned to that revision, its version recorded beside the sampler's.
  - FLORES+, the other lead, meets size and directions (1012 multi-way `devtest` sentences) but fails the licence constraint for this bundle twice: CC-BY-SA 4.0 puts it on the share-alike rung, and its access gate requires agreeing "not to re-host FLORES+ in places where it could be picked up by web crawlers", which a public repository's suite data and rows are. It is not recommended.
  - Original WMT test sets (including the WMT19/WMT20 de-fr news sets, the only WMT source with an original FR<->DE direction) are "freely used for research purposes" only, which is the no-redistribution rung.
- Confidence: high on the licence rung, size and direction set (card metadata at a pinned revision, file listing, aligned rows previewed). Medium on the upstream chain: the English sources are WMT24 test-set sources, which WMT released "for research purposes", and Google's Apache-2.0 declaration over text it did not author is taken at face value (see Assumptions below), a reliance the owner accepted as a disclosed declaration (owner answer Q106 (a), 2026-10-03).
- Remaining uncertainty: the exact count of `is_bad_source` rows and the per-domain counts (the viewer `/filter` endpoint did not answer); neither can drop the pool below 300 distinct segments unless more than about 690 of 998 are bad, which the card's framing (low-quality sources such as HTML, URLs, emojis) does not suggest. The sampler records the post-filter pool size when it draws.

### Evidence, Decisions, Assumptions

- Evidence: the Investigation table above.
- Decisions (already taken): Q106 (a), 2026-10-03, the Apache-2.0 declaration accepted on the permissive rung with the A1 disclosure; the three-rung ladder and its pre-written consequences (epic Decisions row "The licence spike returns one of three verdicts"); per-item licence, source and revision (PRD Methodology 4 and 5); `LICENSE-DATA` section 2 excludes drawn items from CC-BY 4.0; Q41 (a) for any no-redistribution item (not triggered here); Q1 (a), items stored as data through the suite seam.
- Assumptions:
  - A1. The card's `apache-2.0` is the licence of record for both the English `source` and the `target` references. WMT24's own terms say research use; the drawn items' README section and `LICENSE-DATA` section 3 disclose this as a declaration, on the same footing as the two declarations already there (owner answer Q106 (a), 2026-10-03).
  - A2. Apache-2.0 section 4 obligations apply on redistribution: ship a copy of the Apache-2.0 text with the drawn items (the repo has no `NOTICE` to carry over), and state the change made (each item's text is wrapped in the suite's prompt template; the reference is unchanged).
  - A3. The `target` (post-edit) field is the reference, as the card recommends; `fr_FR` is the French variant, not `fr_CA`.
  - A4. FR->DE and DE->EN items have translated (not original) source text, and DE->EN's reference is the original English. FLORES+ and NTREX share this property (all non-English text is translated from English), so it does not discriminate between candidates; the selection rule records it.
  - A5. The PRD open question words the permissive rung as "republishes the drawn items under CC-BY 4.0"; the repository cannot relicense Apache-2.0 text, so "permissive" is read as Methodology 5 and `LICENSE-DATA` section 2 already state it: the item ships under its own permissive licence, recorded per item.
