# Spike: May the model outputs in the published rows be redistributed, and on what terms

## Question

For each model and provider whose output the published bundle carries, do that model's licence and that provider's terms permit redistributing its outputs inside a download whose rows are published under CC-BY 4.0, and does any of them restrict republication, require attribution of its own, or forbid use of outputs for benchmarking?

## Decision

How `LICENSE-DATA` and the export state the model-output part of each row: kept in the download under the author's declaration with the terms found named per model and provider, kept with an added condition the terms impose, or removed from the download for a model or provider whose terms forbid it. This is the epic's falsification clause on "whether generated completions may be redistributed under the bundle's terms", which today is an unverified author declaration.

## Bounds

- Evidence needed: the committed bundle's actual output-bearing fields and their producers (today `predicted_label` from `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` served locally and from `mistral-small-2603` through Mistral's API, read from `aidd_docs/results/quality-reference.jsonl`); for each local roster model, the licence of the weights at the pinned repo revision in `aidd_docs/roster/models.json`, read for any clause on outputs; for each cloud provider (Mistral today, Google AI Studio as the documented second cloud subject), the terms of service in force for the API tier the project uses, read for output ownership, redistribution and benchmark-publication clauses; each text quoted with its URL and retrieval date.
- Stop when: every model and provider that produced an output in the committed bundle has its redistribution position stated from its own terms text, or a term is found that forbids redistribution, which settles the decision for that producer.

### Position per source

"Owned by the publisher" means the terms assign or disclaim the output to the project owner, who may therefore grant CC-BY 4.0 over whatever rights exist in it. A condition is an obligation on the act of publishing, met by a notice.

### Decisions

- `LICENSE-DATA` 1.3 keeps the model-output fields as a separate part outside the CC-BY 4.0 grant: the epic's Boundaries exclude "licensing anything the repository does not own", naming provider outputs (`aidd_docs/backlog/epics/one-download-holds-the-tables-their-licences-and-how-to-cite-them.md`), and the done story `the-data-is-cc-by-4-0-the-code-stays-mit-and-each-says-so-where-it-lives.md` implemented that. This spike does not reopen it; it replaces the unverified declaration with the finding above.
- The first archive carrying open-ended model output waits on this spike (owner answer to Q76, option a, 2026-10-01).
- Google and Mistral are cloud subjects only; GLM and DeepSeek are the judge pair; GPT-5.6 Luna is the calibration judge (`aidd_docs/backlog/epics/any-open-ended-output-carries-two-judges-or-an-honest-flag.md`).

### Assumptions

- A model's output is not the "Work" or a "Derivative Work" of the weights under Apache 2.0, MIT or the LFM Open License, so their redistribution conditions do not attach to it. This reads licences that never mention outputs; it is not a quoted clause.
- A provider use restriction (no competing-model development, no reverse engineering) binds the contracting customer and, for OpenAI, its End Users; a recipient of a CC-BY download is neither, so the restriction does not travel with the data. Were a term to require passing a restriction on, CC-BY 4.0 Section 2(a)(5)(B) (no downstream restrictions) would make that output incompatible with the grant; no term found does.
- The project's Mistral key is held under the Commercial Terms, which name Mistral AI Studio, where API keys are issued. The consumer terms were not readable through the fetch tool; if the account is a consumer account, their output clause is unread.
- DeepSeek's "verify the authenticity and accuracy" duty is met for judge records by publishing them as what the judge returned, labelled AI-generated and possibly wrong, not as the publisher's factual claims. OpenAI's "manually review each generation" sits in the social-media sharing section and is read as not applying to a research dataset.
- Copyright may not subsist in short machine-generated outputs at all; every provider grant is "if any". The finding is that no term stops redistribution, not that a right exists to license.
- This is the author's reading of the quoted texts, not legal advice.

## Outcome

- Result: every model and provider whose output the committed bundle carries (`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` under Apache 2.0, `mistral-small-2603` under Mistral's Commercial Terms) permits redistributing that output, and so does every candidate family, the second cloud subject and the three judges. No source forbids redistribution, requires attribution to itself, restricts benchmark publication, or makes a use restriction travel to recipients. The Decision lands on its second option: kept in the download with an added condition the terms impose. The condition, set by Mistral, Google, DeepSeek, Z.ai and OpenAI and harmless for the open-weight models, is that model-generated content is marked as AI-generated, names the model and provider that produced it, and is not presented as human-written; OpenAI adds attribution of the published content to the owner, which the CC-BY attribution string already gives. `LICENSE-DATA` 1.3 and 3.2 and `aidd_docs/results/NOTICE.md` can replace "unverified declaration" with this per-source finding and that notice.
- Confidence: high on what each text says (quoted, dated, the OpenAI agreement and policy and the LFM licence read in full); medium on the two interpretive steps in Assumptions (outputs are not the licensed Work; customer-side restrictions do not bind dataset recipients), which are the usual reading but not quoted clauses.
- Remaining uncertainty: Mistral's consumer terms unread (relevant only if the key is a consumer account); `qwen3.6-35b-a3b-ud-iq4xs` is pinned to `main`, not a commit, so its licence is read at a moving revision (a roster concern); provider terms change, so a later release re-reads the dated sources above.
